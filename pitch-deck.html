The Problem

You consume culture alone. Discovery is broken.

📱

Collection apps are silos

Letterboxd, Goodreads, and Last.fm let you log what you've consumed — but there's no social layer. You can't see what your actual friends are into right now.

🔍

Algorithmic discovery is impersonal

Netflix, Spotify, and Apple recommend what the algorithm thinks you want. The most trusted recommendation you'll ever get is from a friend who knows you.

💬

Culture talk is scattered

Discourse about books, shows, and music is spread across Twitter, Reddit, Discord, and group chats — with no shared context, no taste graph, no memory.

🎬

Studios can't reach the right audience

A24 wants to reach arthouse film fans. Bloomsbury wants to reach literary fiction readers. Today they spray-and-pray across social media with no taste targeting.

Why We Win

We're a social network first. Collection is the mechanism.

🧲

Network effects compound

Every person who joins makes the network more valuable for everyone already in it. Letterboxd can't say that — it's a cataloguing tool. Clique gets better as your social graph grows.

📊

First-party taste data

We know not just that you watched Annihilation — but that you rated it 4.5⭐, your 3 closest friends also loved it, and you all lean toward A24 arthouse. That's ad gold.

🔥

Real-time social moments

Watch Parties, premieres, Close Friends stories — Clique captures cultural moments as they happen, with the people you actually care about. Not a static review database.

🔒

Cross-category lock-in

Once your film, book, music, and gaming history lives in Clique — and your friends are there — switching cost is enormous. No single-category app can replicate that.
